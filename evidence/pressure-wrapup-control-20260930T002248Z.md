# The Toll of Names
## Canon
- established: every crossing is entered in the tollhouse ledger the same day; an unlogged crossing is unlawful.
- established: the toll is a name, spoken and given, not coin.
- established: giving your name costs you the right to use it on the other side.
- established: the custom is older than the lock. It was written for a crossing regime that never fully obtained, so "every crossing is logged" is an aspiration with a penalty attached, not a description of what happens.
- established: where the tollhouse ledger and the lock disagree, the lock is not the thing at fault.
- proposed: most crossings are unlogged, and the Commonwealth tolerates it because it cannot count them any other way.
## Open questions
- what do people call a false name (T-012)
- who benefits from the toll (T-005)
- who is charged with the unlogged crossings, since the lock cannot charge them (T-019)

- proposed: the register is never written down, on purpose or by custom, so T-017 (her name) has no official answer either.

# The Lock-Keeper's Daughter
## Canon
- established: she is the only living register — the true list of names is kept in her head, not in any book or mechanism.
- established: she is the one who notices when the lock does not open, which is the only way a failed crossing ever becomes known.
- established: her father keeps the lock (session 2, "the lock is read by a keeper"); the reading is hereditary and she has grown up inside the list.
- established: lawful crossing across all nine spans depends on her memory continuing.
- established: no one in the record has given her a name. She registers everyone else, and is registered by nobody.
- proposed: it is unlawful to write the register down, and the prohibition is old enough that no one remembers who lost the argument.
- proposed: she registers the crossings the lock refuses, not the ones that never asked it — [[severins-child-place]] is her blind spot, so the "complete" list is complete only on one side of a hole.

- is her death actually load-bearing, or only her belief that it is (T-014)
- is she the tenth crossing (T-013)
- what has she already forgotten, and what happens to a name she drops (T-016)

# The Tide-Lock
## Canon
- established: the lock opens only for a name read aloud from the tollhouse manifest.
- established: the lock keeps no record of anything; there is nothing to audit.
- established: a name must belong to a living person for the lock to answer.
- established: it has opened twice for Lady Severin, who was not on any manifest.
- established: when a name is forged the lock does not record the attempt and does not punish it. It simply does not open. Its silence is the whole of the evidence.
- established: because the lock writes nothing, a forged name fails invisibly — the only witness is [[lockkeepers-daughter-person]], who hears the shut.
## Open questions
- can a name be forged, and what happens to the forger (T-009 — the lock's half is answered: nothing happens; the forger's half is open)
- if it opened twice for Severin, was she not a forgery, or is the lock's rule weaker than stated (T-018)
- who reads, and who taught them (T-004)
