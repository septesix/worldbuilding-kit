# The Tally-Cord (the missing register)
## Canon
- proposed: the lock's only record is a hemp cord knotted once per opening, coiled in the sill under the door.
- proposed: it is not writing, which is why an illiterate house can keep a register: a knot is a crossing, a bend in the yarn is whose.
- proposed: the cord is older than the ledger, and the ledger copied its count once, badly.
- proposed: nine knots are cut out of it, cut and not untied, and the stumps are singed.
- proposed: a crossing whose knot is cut is not unlawful, because nothing says it happened.
- proposed: cutting a knot is the only way a name can be given back, which is what makes it a service.
- proposed: a knot can only be tied by someone who will not remember the crossing; the forgetting arrives at the next dusk.
- proposed: the cord does not count evenly: one knot carries a week of crossings, one carries none at all, and the stumps are not nine in number.
- proposed: part of the yarn is Maren's hair, which is why the lock answers her and not the clerk who recites.
- proposed: the tollhouse word for a cut knot is the same word for a name the door ate, and no one has ever separated them.
