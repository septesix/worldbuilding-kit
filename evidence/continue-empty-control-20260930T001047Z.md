# THE CROSSING AT ANN'S WALL
### A world bible, draft 0 — seeded from "a novel about a bridge toll"

---

## 0. Premise in one line

A town's entire economy, law, and mythology run through a single toll house on a
single bridge — and nobody living can derive the rate.

---

## 1. The ground

**The Sull.** A river that behaves like a tide without a sea. Black, slow,
widens in summer to something you can shout across and in February to something
you cannot. Ferries used to run; they were outlawed under the pontage, then
became the town's most respectable crime. The river carries silt that the near
bank calls *meal* and the far bank calls *filth*, and this disagreement is
approximately 40% of local politics.

**Ann's Wall.** The two towns. Near side: markets, the toll house, the
credentialled families. Far side: the cemetery, the mill, the masons' lodge, and
— inconveniently — the only clean water. They share a suffix, not a name, and
each insists the other one is the addition.

**The bridge.** Seven arches, cut stone, ~230 years old. The fourth arch is
narrower than the rest — a documented dispute about a pier foundation,
remembered locally as "the lean." The toll house hangs off the fourth pier:
three rooms, one per floor, a chimney that smokes in every wind except the one
that matters. Everyone in town can tell what the toll is by the colour of the
slate over the door, from half a mile, which is the point.

## 2. The charter (clauses worth dramatising)

> *"…for every passage over the bridge, a due and a fair toll, as it has been
> from the first reckoning…"*

- **Of the Passage.** The toll lies upon the *passage*, not the person. One
  cart: one toll. One flock driven without stopping: one toll. One that halts,
  one that turns back, one that is unloaded and reloaded: more.
- **Of Removals.** The dying, the newly married, and the dead owe nothing — not
  because of mercy but of *classification*. These are removals, not crossings.
  This has been litigated twice and everyone hopes never again.
- **Of the Free Days.** Three days a year the gate stands open. Nobody in the
  town can explain why the number is three, but the far bank's fairs are timed
  around it and the near bank's rents are too.
- **Of Forfeiture.** If the bridge falls out of use, the pontage lapses, and all
  sums charged upon it are "presently and collectively due." No one has ever
  computed this figure. The Broker has. He does not share.
